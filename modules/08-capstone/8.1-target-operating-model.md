# 8.1 The Target Operating Model — 11 Stages

- The target operating model is the end-to-end workflow to work toward: requirement intake, codebase exploration, feature mapping, technical specification, human plan approval, implementation, verification, review, correction loop, human final review, QA handoff.
- The stages fall into three groups: understanding before changing (1–4), changing under control (6–9), and human accountability at the gates (5, 10) and at the handoff (11).
- Each stage consumes the previous stage's artifact and produces its own — clarified requirements, exploration notes, a feature map, a specification, an approval, a diff, a verification report, review findings, a handoff document.
- Every stage draws on an earlier level: context and acceptance criteria (Level 1), Claude Code mechanisms (Level 2), context packages and skills (Level 3), loops and verification (Level 4), delegation and independent review (Level 5), specifications, evaluation, and security (Level 6).
- The two human gates are deliberate. Plan approval is the cheapest moment to correct direction; final review is the last moment before others depend on the result.
- AI may perform many of the steps; the human engineer remains accountable for requirements, architecture, risk acceptance, and final delivery.
- The model scales down. A small fix collapses several stages into minutes, but still has a stated goal, a check, and a review. Skipping the thinking is different from shortening the ceremony.
- It is a target, not a mandate: adopt stages in the order your failures suggest — most teams gain first from intake, verification, and the correction loop.
