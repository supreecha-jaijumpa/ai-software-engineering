# 8.4 Capstone Brief — Creator Product Submission

- Build a practical AI Software Engineering System that supports a full-stack feature from requirement to QA handoff, and prove it by delivering one feature through it.
- The suggested scenario: a creator creates a product; the product is saved as a draft or submitted for review; an administrator reviews and approves or rejects it; approved products become available for sale; the system records status changes and relevant audit information.
- The scenario is chosen because it exercises everything: requirements, data modeling, API contracts, UI, authorization, tests, edge cases, review, and verification.
- It is small on the surface and rich underneath: a status state machine with allowed and forbidden transitions, two roles with different permissions, an audit trail, and questions the requirement leaves open — can a rejected product be resubmitted, can an approved one be edited?
- Required capabilities of the system: requirement analysis and clarification; codebase exploration; feature map and impact analysis; technical planning and human approval; frontend and backend implementation; test planning and execution.
- And further: independent code review; a verification loop with bounded retries; persistent task state and recovery; a final report and QA handoff; workflow metrics and token/cost awareness; permission and security controls.
- The product being graded is the workflow, not the feature. A perfect feature produced by ad-hoc prompting does not pass; a modest feature produced by a system that plans, verifies, stops safely, and reports honestly does.
- You may substitute a scenario from your own project if it is comparable — full-stack, with roles, state changes, and real edge cases — and the mentor agrees.
