# 4.4 Bounded Iteration, Retry Policy & Stop Conditions

- An unbounded loop pursuing a vague goal is the classic failure of agentic work: the agent keeps editing, costs accumulate, and the code drifts further from a clean state with each attempt.
- A bound is a limit set before the loop starts: maximum attempts per failure, maximum total iterations, a time or token budget, or a limit on how much of the codebase may change.
- A stop condition defines when the loop ends. Success: measurable exit criteria are met. Failure: the bound is reached, the same failure repeats, no progress is being made, or the problem is outside the agreed scope.
- A retry policy says what may change between attempts. Retrying the same action unchanged makes sense only for transient failures; a logic failure needs a new diagnosis and a different approach.
- Detect non-progress explicitly: the same test failing with the same message after two different fixes means the diagnosis is wrong, and more attempts will not help.
- Each failed attempt leaves residue — partial edits, misleading context. After the bound is hit, returning to a known good state is often better than building on the wreckage.
- The right bound depends on cost and risk: generous for cheap, reversible steps with clear feedback; strict for expensive or hard-to-reverse ones. Two or three attempts per distinct failure is a common starting point, not a law.
- Hitting a bound is a correct outcome, not a defect. A workflow that stops and reports "blocked after three attempts, here is what I tried" has worked as designed.
