# 4.6 Maker-Checker Patterns

- Maker-checker separates the party that produces a result from the party that verifies it. It is an old control from finance and operations, and it applies directly to AI workflows.
- The reason: whoever made something is the worst-placed to check it. An agent reviewing its own change re-reads its own reasoning and tends to confirm it.
- Independence has degrees: the same session re-reading its work (weak), a fresh context given only the requirement and the diff (better), a different role with different instructions and tools (better still), deterministic checks and human review (strongest).
- What the checker is given matters: the requirement, the acceptance criteria, and the result — not the maker's explanation of why it is correct.
- The checker must be allowed to fail the work and must report evidence. A checker instructed to "confirm that it works" will confirm it.
- A checker that only reads cannot break anything; keeping it read-only also keeps roles clear — the checker reports, the maker fixes.
- Maker-checker costs a second pass, in tokens and time. Use it where an undetected error is expensive, and skip it for trivial, easily reversed changes.
- It does not remove the human: the checker reduces what reaches human review and raises its quality, but accountability for the result stays with the engineer.
