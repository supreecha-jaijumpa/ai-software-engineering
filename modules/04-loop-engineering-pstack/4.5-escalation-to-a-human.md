# 4.5 Escalation to a Human

- Escalation is the loop handing a decision back to a person. It is part of the design, with defined triggers — not an apology for failure.
- Three triggers: blocked (cannot proceed — missing access, failing dependency, retry bound reached), risky (the next action is destructive, irreversible, or outside the agreed scope), and uncertain (requirements are ambiguous or evidence conflicts).
- The alternative to escalating is guessing. A guessed requirement produces work that looks finished and is wrong — the most expensive kind of error, because it is discovered late.
- A good escalation is cheap to act on: what the goal was, what was tried, the evidence, the specific decision needed, the options with their consequences, and a recommendation.
- A poor escalation transfers the whole problem ("it doesn't work, what should I do?") or asks about things the agent could have looked up itself.
- Calibration matters in both directions: too many questions and the human becomes the bottleneck and stops reading them; too few and the agent makes decisions that were not its to make.
- Approval gates are planned escalations placed at known points of high leverage — before implementation starts, before a schema change, before anything outward-facing.
- Work that does not depend on the answer can continue while waiting; work that does must stop. The state file should record that the task is blocked and on what.
