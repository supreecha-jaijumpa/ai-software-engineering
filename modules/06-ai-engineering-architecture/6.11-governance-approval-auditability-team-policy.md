# 6.11 Governance — Approval Policies, Auditability & Team Policy

- Governance is the set of agreed rules about how AI is used in engineering work: what agents may do alone, what needs a human, how it is recorded, and who is accountable.
- A human-approval policy classifies actions by risk. Typical categories needing approval: destructive operations (deleting data, force-pushing, dropping tables), high-impact changes (schema migrations, authentication and payment code, infrastructure), and anything outward-facing (deploying, publishing, sending messages).
- Write the policy as enforceable configuration wherever possible — permission rules, hooks, branch protection, CI gates — and as a short document for what cannot be automated.
- Approval must be meaningful. A reviewer approving thirty prompts an hour, or a plan nobody read, is a ritual; fewer, better-placed gates give more real control.
- Auditability means being able to reconstruct afterwards what was done, by which workflow, on whose instruction, and with what verification — through commit history, task records, verification reports, and logs.
- Team policies make practice consistent: shared project configuration under review, rules for adding MCP servers and plugins, what data may be sent to which tools, and how AI-assisted changes are reviewed.
- Accountability does not transfer. The engineer who merges a change owns it, whoever or whatever wrote it — so review standards for AI-written code are at least those for human-written code.
- Govern in proportion to risk: a solo prototype needs a few sensible defaults; a team shipping to production needs explicit, enforced policy. Review the policy when incidents or new capabilities show it is out of date.
