# 6.2 Harness Controls — Sandboxing, Quality Gates & Oversight

- Controls are the parts of the harness that limit and check what the agent does. They differ in strength: an instruction asks, a permission rule decides, a sandbox makes the action impossible.
- Sandboxing restricts what executed commands can reach — which directories they may write to, whether they can access the network — at the operating-system or container level, so the limit holds even if a command is not what it appears to be.
- A sandbox also allows more autonomy with less risk: inside a boundary you trust, fewer approval prompts are needed. Check the current documentation for what your platform supports.
- Deterministic quality gates are scripts that must pass before work moves on: format, lint, type check, build, tests, secret scanning. They can run as hooks, as steps in a skill, and again in CI.
- Gates belong in more than one place. A check the agent runs locally can be skipped or misreported; the same check in CI is outside the agent's control.
- Human oversight is a control with limited capacity. Spend it where judgment is needed — plans, risky actions, final review — and automate everything a script can decide.
- Layer the controls (defense in depth): instructions reduce how often a bad action is attempted, permissions and hooks stop most of the rest, the sandbox contains what gets through, review catches what was contained but wrong.
- Every control has a cost in friction or speed. A control that is routinely bypassed is worse than none, because it reports safety that does not exist.
