# 6.9 Observability & Reliability

- Observability is the ability to explain what a workflow did and why from the records it leaves. Reliability is behaving predictably — including failing predictably — across many runs.
- Execution traces and logs record the sequence: stages, tool calls, commands and their results, decisions, and stop reasons. They are what you read when a run goes wrong.
- Tool-call and failure tracking shows where time and tokens go and which steps fail most. A stage that fails in a third of all runs is a design problem, not bad luck.
- Retry and recovery policies make failure handling explicit: what is retried, how often, from which state, and what happens when the limit is reached.
- Timeouts and bounded execution guarantee that every run ends — in success, in a classified failure, or in an escalation — never in an endless loop or a hung command.
- Reproducible verification means the final checks can be re-run by anyone, from a script, with the same result. A verification that only the agent could perform, once, is not evidence.
- Failure classification feeds improvement: counting failures by class shows whether to fix the context, the tools, the gates, or the task definitions.
- Human escalation is part of reliability — a workflow that stops and asks at the right moment is more reliable than one that always produces an answer. Keep observability light: a structured log and saved verification reports are enough to begin with.
