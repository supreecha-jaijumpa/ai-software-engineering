# 1.8 Agentic AI — Chat vs Action-Oriented Workflows

- In chat, the model produces text and a human decides what to do with it. In an agentic workflow, the model chooses and executes a sequence of actions toward a goal, observing the result of each one.
- An agent is roughly: a model, a set of tools, a loop, and a stopping condition. Its quality depends at least as much on the loop and the tools as on the model.
- Agentic work shifts your job from writing each step to defining the goal, the boundaries, and the checks — and reviewing the outcome.
- Autonomy multiplies both value and risk: an agent can finish a multi-file change unattended, and it can also make twenty wrong edits before anyone looks.
- Errors compound across steps. A wrong assumption in step two shapes everything after it, so early verification is cheaper than late review.
- An agent needs things a chat does not: a clear definition of done, permission limits, a way to observe real results (tests, builds), and a rule for when to stop and ask.
- Not everything should be agentic. A quick question, a small edit, or a decision that needs your judgment is faster and safer as a direct exchange.
- Practical consequence: before delegating a task, state the goal, what the agent may touch, how success is measured, and when it must stop.
