# 5.10 Maker-Checker & Independent Review at Scale

- With several agents producing work, independent review becomes more important, not less: errors can now be introduced in parallel and can hide in the gaps between tasks.
- Independence is the property to protect. A reviewer that shares the maker's context, brief, or reasoning will repeat the maker's blind spots.
- Give the reviewer the requirement, the contract, and the diff — not the implementer's summary of why the change is right.
- Specialized reviews find different things: correctness and maintainability, security, performance, test quality. Running them in parallel on the same read-only diff is a safe and effective use of multiple agents.
- Several reviewers produce overlapping and sometimes contradictory findings. Someone must merge, de-duplicate, and rank them — and verify the important ones before acting.
- Reviewers produce false positives. A finding is a hypothesis until confirmed by reading the code or by a failing test; fixing unverified findings creates new defects.
- Review each part and the integrated whole. Per-task review misses problems that exist only after the parts are combined.
- The chain of checks is deterministic gates → agent review → human review. Agent review narrows and sharpens what the human sees; it does not replace the human's accountability for the merge.
