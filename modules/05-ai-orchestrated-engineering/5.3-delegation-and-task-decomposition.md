# 5.3 Delegation & Task Decomposition

- Decomposition is splitting a goal into tasks that can be completed and verified separately. Delegation is handing one of them to another agent together with everything it needs.
- A well-formed task is bounded (clear scope and stop point), independent (does not need a running conversation with other tasks), and verifiable (it has its own acceptance criteria).
- Split along natural seams — module boundaries, layers with a contract between them, read-only research versus change — not by arbitrary size.
- A delegation brief must stand alone, because the worker has none of your context: the goal and why it matters, the scope and what is out of scope, what is already known or ruled out, relevant files, constraints, and the output format.
- If you cannot write a clear brief, you do not yet understand the task well enough to delegate it — do the thinking first.
- Delegation has overhead: writing the brief, the worker's start-up exploration, reading and checking the result. For small or tightly coupled tasks, doing the work directly is faster and more accurate.
- Do not delegate understanding. The orchestrator needs to grasp the result well enough to judge it; a summary of something you cannot evaluate is not progress.
- Ask for verifiable results — findings with file references, changes with test evidence — and state plainly that reporting "blocked" or "not found" is an acceptable answer.
