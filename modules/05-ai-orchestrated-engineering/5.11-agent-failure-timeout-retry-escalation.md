# 5.11 Agent Failure, Timeout, Retry & Escalation

- In a multi-agent workflow failures are normal events to be designed for: an agent may return nothing useful, return something wrong, run too long, or stop halfway.
- Failure modes to expect: misunderstanding the brief, drifting out of scope, reporting success without evidence, exhausting its context, hanging on a command, and failing because its environment was incomplete.
- The orchestrator cannot assume a returned result is a correct result. Check the output against the contract — structure, evidence, scope — before using it.
- Timeouts and budgets bound each worker: a limit on time, turns, or tokens. A worker that reaches the limit should report what it has, not be left running.
- Retry with a reason. A failure caused by a poor brief is fixed by a better brief; a transient failure by trying again; a failure caused by an impossible or ill-defined task is not fixed by retrying at all.
- Limit retries per task and overall, and record each attempt. Three agents each retrying three times on a flawed plan is nine times the waste.
- Partial failure needs a rule: when one of four parallel tasks fails, decide whether the others' results are still valid or depended on the failed one.
- Escalation follows the Level 4 triggers — blocked, risky, uncertain — and is aggregated by the orchestrator: one clear question to the human, not four interleaved ones from four agents.
